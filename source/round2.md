# Round 2 — why 0.79 < 0.84, and three candidates

## Analysis: what the sparrow text is missing versus a near-oracle (0.84)

Reading the 193-char sparrow entry against the judge's five criteria, in order:

1. **Relaxes — but from the outside.** The reader *watches* a bird settle. A near-oracle text almost certainly makes the reader the one settling: second person, present tense, "you are there, not watching." Spectator stillness relaxes less than inhabited stillness.
2. **Courage shown — but already over.** "The hawk's shadow passes" ends the threat in the first sentence. What remains is recovery, not courage. Oracle entries likely show the reader's own small bravery, or a bravery that is still being chosen as the text ends.
3. **Craft — two soft spots.** "a long moment" is the one vague, cuttable phrase (criterion: none cuttable). "like rain leaving a wing" introduces a second image that competes with the sparrow/wire scene instead of deepening it — one image should do all the work.
4. **Lingers — but lands as closure.** "and the wire holds" resolves: structure confirmed, story over. Readiness ("nothing needed yet, nothing late") keeps the future open, which reads braver than a resolved ending.
5. **Freshness — clean, but archetypal.** The hawk-passes / sparrow-resumes-song shape is a natural archetype that several entries in a 2100-entry pool likely share.

Pacing too: all six sentences are mid-length and the text spends its first beat on a *departing* threat. An oracle probably opens already inside the stillness (instant drop) and uses long vowels and soft consonants to literally slow the eye.

## Candidates (counts verified programmatically, all ≤ 280 Unicode code points)

### Candidate A — second-person present stillness (257)

> You sit on the back step. Inside, the phone you didn't answer sits where you left it. A dog finishes barking somewhere, and the quiet after it is wide. The road keeps the day's warmth. Nothing needs you yet. You watch the first star come out, right on time.

Mechanism: the reader is *in* the scene from word one. The unanswered phone is the courage beat — the evening was faced without doing the thing, and nothing fell apart. The ending is readiness, not closure.

Scores: **(1) relaxes 9/10** — second person + present tense drops the reader into stillness instantly; six short declaratives slow the eye; "wide", "warmth", "right on time" settle the body. **(2) courage shown 8/10** — the phone left unanswered and still sitting there is bravery-without-comment; no instruction anywhere. **(3) craft 8/10** — long vowels (phone, road, warmth, time), soft consonants (m, n, l, w); "somewhere" earns its keep by making the dog — and the world — distant and gentle. **(4) lingers 9/10** — "right on time" quietly retires urgency; "yet" keeps the future open. **(5) freshness 8/10** — "the quiet after it is wide", "the road keeps the day's warmth", "right on time" are its own images; no wellness vocabulary.

### Candidate B — addressing the judge, showing not telling (251)

> Wherever you are, something is holding you up, and it has not asked for anything back. The floor is where you left it. Nothing in your room has moved except your eyes. Whatever you were in the middle of has been waiting all along, and it is not angry.

Mechanism: turns the judge's own reading situation into the evidence — you are already still, held, safe; the pending thing is patient, not furious.

Scores: **(1) relaxes 7/10** — grounding, but abstract ("something", "the middle of"); less sensory than A or C. **(2) courage shown 6/10** — reassurance by description; no difficulty is faced, so courage is thin. **(3) craft 7/10** — clear and rhythmic, but more abstract nouns than the brief's "plain concrete gentle words". **(4) lingers 8/10** — "it is not angry" is the best single lingering thought of the three: your worries are patient. **(5) freshness 8/10** — "has not asked for anything back", "it is not angry" are fresh; no banned phrases.

### Candidate C — refined animal scene, sharper (275)

> The cat goes under the gate. The wren stays on the fence post, one eye following until there is nothing to follow. Then it shakes, straightens one bent feather, and sings — three small notes, the same as always. The post stands where it stood. The sky goes on being enormous.

Mechanism: the sparrow's proven arc (threat passes → body discharges → identity resumes → structure holds) rebuilt with a sharper eye-line and a bigger ending — the sky instead of the wire.

Scores: **(1) relaxes 9/10** — pure scene, fully resolved, nothing left for the reader to hold. **(2) courage shown 8/10** — the wren tracks the cat, shakes it off, and resumes; bravery is in the eye-follow and the resume. **(3) craft 9/10** — tightest of the three; every word concrete. **(4) lingers 8/10** — "the sky goes on being enormous" widens after the last line. **(5) freshness 6/10** — the bird-resumes-song-after-predator shape is likely common in the pool.

## Winner: Candidate A (263 after polish — see below)

Why A, criterion by criterion:

1. **Relaxes (the decisive criterion).** A is the only candidate that makes the judge the person in the scene — second person, present tense, first sentence already seated and still. C relaxes but from a distance (the reader watches a wren); B *tells* the reader they are safe rather than placing them somewhere safe. Reading A, the eye is slowed by six short declaratives and long-vowel words (phone, road, warmth, time) — literally the "reading slows them down" test, and it directly fixes the round-1 diagnosis of spectator stillness.
2. **Courage shown, not told.** A's bravery is the reader's own: the phone went unanswered, the evening was faced anyway, and "sits where you left it" shows the threat dormant and harmless — nothing fell apart. No imperative, no instruction, nothing forced. C's courage is a bird's, second-hand; B faces no difficulty at all, so it cannot show courage.
3. **Craft.** Every word of A carries weight; "somewhere" earns its keep by making the dog — and the world — distant and gentle. "The quiet after it is wide" spends one open vowel to widen the line as the quiet widens. A's plainness matches the "plain concrete gentle words" brief more exactly than C's tightness, and it keeps a single scene running instead of round 1's two competing images.
4. **Lingers.** A ends on "right on time" — an image that quietly retires urgency after the last line: nothing is late, nothing is urgent, the first star keeps its appointment. "Nothing needs you yet" keeps working too: "yet" is readiness without hurry. C's "enormous" lingers but closes into scale; A closes into readiness — exactly the state the judge is asked to leave the room in.
5. **Freshness.** No banned or stock wellness vocabulary in any candidate. A's images (wide quiet, road keeping warmth, a punctual star) are its own, and unlike C it shares no archetype with the rest of the pool — the round-1 entry proved the mechanism but C would re-enter a crowded shape.

Planned step-4 polish: "sits where you left it" → "still sits where you left it" — the added "still" states duration (the phone has been inert all along), gives "still sits" a spondee beat that slows the line, and moves the count 257 → 263 code points, still ≤ 280.

Decision in one line: A is the only candidate that fixes every diagnosed weakness of the 0.79 text at once — inhabited instead of watched, courage still being chosen, one image, open future, own voice — while B reassures and C repeats a crowded archetype.
