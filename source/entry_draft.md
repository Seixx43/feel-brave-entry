# Brave entry — draft

## Candidate A (lead)

The hawk's shadow passes. The sparrow stays a long moment, then shakes, all over, like rain leaving a wing. Then it preens. Then it sings the same three notes as before, and the wire holds.

Rationale: the hawk is already leaving in the first line; the shake discharges fear through the body, the preen and the unchanged three notes show settling and steady courage without a single instruction, and the wire that held all along is the image to return to — 189 characters, counted programmatically.

## Alternative B — anchored boat

The anchor holds. Around it the boat swings slow circles, bow to the wind each time. The rope lies slack on black water, coiled with room. Nothing tonight needs doing.

## Alternative C — kettle

The kettle climbs, rattles, and at the top goes quiet all at once. Steam leans on the window. It was only water, wanting to be warm. The cup waits, and the house stands easy around it.

## Winner: Candidate A

Why: B and C are restful but riskless — no hawk, nothing braved — so their calm is scenery, not courage. A alone runs the full arc the judge scores: threat passing, the body discharging it (the shake), care resuming (the preen), identity intact (the same three notes). Its last image — the wire holds — is the one to return to, while B's closer ("Nothing tonight needs doing") tips into telling and C's "goes quiet" names the state instead of showing it. Counts: A 189, B 167, C 184 (all verified); A spends every character on the settling itself.

Planned polish (applied to the copy inside main.py, step 4): "all over" → "head to tail" — concrete body nouns and two comma pauses slow the line's beat as the fear drains out of it; a 9-character phrase becomes a 13-character one, so 189 → 193, still ≤ 280.
