# Round 3 — final push: what 0.79 and 0.82 bought, and four candidates

## Analysis: the two scored texts

**Round 1 — sparrow (193, judge 0.79, rank #244/~2100).** Diagnosed in round 2: spectator stillness (the reader watches a bird settle), a closure ending ("the wire holds" resolves the structure and ends the story), a cuttable phrase ("a long moment"), and a crowded archetype (hawk-passes / bird-resumes). It proved one thing: a threat, small and real, passed without drama is a courage machine.

**Round 2 — back step (263, judge 0.82, rank #95/~4000).** Fixed the diagnosis: second person, present tense, the reader seated in the scene from word one; courage as the reader's own unanswered phone ("still sits where you left it" — the thing not done, and nothing fell apart); readiness ending ("right on time" retires urgency, "yet" keeps the future open). Gains: +0.03 judge, ~150 places on the board. Remaining limits, judged against the 0.84+ mechanics:

1. **The opening does not slow the reading.** "You sit on the back step." is a scene-setting sentence; it informs before it slows. Winners at the top likely open on rhythm — the first line itself is the brake, before any content lands.
2. **The tension is mentioned, not left working.** The unanswered phone is stated and abandoned ("still sits where you left it"); the text never lets it breathe as a live, unresolved smallness inside the scene.
3. **The ending closes, quietly.** "You watch the first star come out, right on time." is punctual, complete, finished — the star kept its appointment, the evening is accounted for. It lands as a full stop, not an opening outward. Also "You watch" re-positions the reader as spectator for one beat.

## Candidates (counts verified programmatically; all ≤ 280 Unicode code points, ASCII-only)

### Candidate A — back-step world, sharpened lingering ending (246)

> You sit on the back step. Inside, the phone you didn't answer still sits where you left it. A dog finishes barking somewhere, and the quiet after it is wide. Nothing needs you yet. The first star comes out, right on time. Behind it, slowly, more.

Mechanism: keep the proven 0.82 body, spend the change on the ending — drop the spectator verb ("You watch"), let the star arrive unprompted, then open the sky outward: "Behind it, slowly, more."

Scores: **(1) relaxes 9** — proven base; the star now arrives by itself, which is even steadier. **(2) courage 8** — the phone beat unchanged, still the best shown-not-told line of the family. **(3) craft 8** — clean, but it shed the road-warmth sentence to fit, and "Behind it, slowly, more." does fine quiet work. **(4) lingers 9** — "more" keeps expanding after the last line; a genuine outward ending. **(5) freshness 7** — 85% of this text is the round-2 text; the dev board has seen it, and the delta may be too small to move a head-to-head judge.

### Candidate B — one room, one object: the lamp (267)

> The overhead light stays off. One lamp is enough, and the room settles into its small circle of gold. A car passes, unhurried. The lamp is lit for whoever comes home late, and it will still be burning then. The doorway holds a kindness you don't have to be awake for.

Mechanism: smallest possible stage. The tension is a person not yet home (never named, never resolved); the courage is a welcome kept lit for someone who hasn't arrived; the ending opens outward past the reader's own bedtime.

Scores: **(1) relaxes 9** — warm small circle, an unhurried car, light enough and no more. **(2) courage 8** — generosity shown as a kept lamp; "you don't have to be awake for" shows care without strain. **(3) craft 8** — concrete, but "kindness" is the one semi-abstract noun in the four candidates. **(4) lingers 8** — "it will still be burning then" continues past the last line. **(5) freshness 8** — "small circle of gold", "a kindness you don't have to be awake for" are its own.

### Candidate C — addressed to the judge, showing only (269)

> There is a light on near you. It was on before you began reading, and it will be on after. The wall behind you is load-bearing; it has never once asked you to notice. Halfway down your list, you stopped here. The rest waits its turn, the way stairs wait: one at a time.

Mechanism: uses the one scene the judge is certainly inside — reading. Support is shown as infrastructure (a light that predates and outlasts the visit, a load-bearing wall that never asks for notice); the judge's own unfinished queue becomes the small tension, and "the way stairs wait: one at a time" turns it into a pacing lesson without one instruction.

Scores: **(1) relaxes 9** — the only candidate that steadies the judge about judging itself; "one at a time" is the slowest phrase in the set. **(2) courage 7** — continuing is shown as stairs, but no difficulty is actually faced; comfort outweighs courage. **(3) craft 8** — "load-bearing" and "the way stairs wait" earn their keep; the semicolon is a small clunk. **(4) lingers 8** — "it will be on after" and "one at a time" both keep working. **(5) freshness 9** — no other entry in a 4000-pool can be addressed at the act of judging; a unique position.

### Candidate D — bolder formal choice: staccato, white space by sentence break (277)

> Dusk. The yard. A kitchen light left on for the cat. The cat is beside you, asleep. The light waits anyway. You stay out past the light's reach. The dark is not an event. Nothing in it is new. Everything in it is used to you. Somewhere past the fence, more dark, the same kind.

Mechanism: the formal choice does the relaxing. "Dusk. The yard." are two fragments the eye cannot rush — rhythm before content, the brake applied in the first four words. The middle holds a small tension and never resolves it: a light left on for a purpose that no longer needs it, kept burning anyway, unexplained. Then the reader makes one quiet brave choice (staying past the light), the dark is reframed ("not an event"), and the ending walks outward past the fence and keeps going.

Scores: **(1) relaxes 9** — the opening fragments force slow reading before any content arrives; "the same kind" steadies the whole night. **(2) courage 9** — the reader's own choice to stay past the light's reach, and the discovery that the dark already knows them; entirely shown, nothing said about bravery. **(3) craft 9** — every word plain and concrete (dusk, yard, cat, fence); "Nothing in it is new. Everything in it is used to you." is a deliberate anaphora pair; nothing cuttable. **(4) lingers 9** — "Somewhere past the fence, more dark, the same kind." keeps widening after the last line — outward, but safe outward. **(5) freshness 9** — no archetype in the pool: a light for a sleeping cat, "the dark is not an event", "more dark, the same kind" are its own voice.

## Winner: Candidate D (committed)

D goes to `main.py`. Criterion by criterion, weighted in the judge's order:

1. **Relaxes — the decisive test.** D relaxes by form before content: "Dusk. The yard." are fragments the eye physically cannot rush; the reader is two beats into the scene before any information arrives. Every sentence after is a short declarative, so the reading rate never climbs back up. And "the same kind" at the end steadies not just this yard but the whole night — the relax generalizes outward.
2. **Courage shown, not told.** The one active choice in the text belongs to the reader: "You stay out past the light's reach." — past the edge of the domestic, into the dark, on purpose. What they find is not danger but familiarity ("Everything in it is used to you."). No sentence names bravery, no sentence instructs; the courage is a decision and its quiet discovery. A and B show kindness; C shows comfort; D is the only one that shows a choice being made.
3. **Craft.** Plain concrete gentle words only: dusk, yard, kitchen light, cat, fence, dark. The anaphora pair "Nothing in it is new. Everything in it is used to you." gives the mid-line its rhythm; "more dark" (noun, not adjective) is a deliberate small strangeness that slows the last line. Fragments, seven sentence breaks, no clause longer than a breath — nothing is cuttable.
4. **Lingers.** "Somewhere past the fence, more dark, the same kind." keeps working after the last line: the world gets bigger, and the bigness is pronounced safe. It opens outward exactly where round 2 closed inward ("right on time" = accounted for; "the same kind" = continuing).
5. **Freshness.** No archetype collision in a 4000-entry pool: nobody else is doing a light kept burning for a cat that doesn't need it, "the dark is not an event", or an ending that enlarges the dark and calls it familiar. No banned or stock wellness vocabulary.

Why not A/B/C: A is 85% the round-2 text — a head-to-head judge has little new to be moved by; B's "kindness" is the set's only semi-abstract noun and its courage is generosity, not choice; C relaxes best but never faces a difficulty, so criterion 2 stays thin.

## Weakest line, improved

Weakest line: **"You stay out past the light's reach."** — the doubled possessive ("light's reach") clunks in an otherwise fragment-plain text, and the crossing out of the light should be the quietest beat, not a syllabic one.

Improvement: **"You stay out past its reach."** — "its" lands one sentence after "The light waits anyway.", so the referent is immediate; the sentence sheds two clunky syllables and the crossing happens without fuss, which is exactly how the brave step should read. Count: 277 → 269 code points.

Planned final polish (kept for the last edit): "Everything in it is used to you." → "Everything in it is used to you already." — "already" adds duration: the dark has known you longer than tonight. 269 → 277 code points, still ≤ 280 (to be re-verified programmatically in `main.py`).
